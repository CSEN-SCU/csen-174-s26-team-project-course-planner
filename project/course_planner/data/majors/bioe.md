### BIOE 21 — Introduction to Physiology

Units: 4

This course will cover five anatomical systems and how the structure of the human body relates to and defines its function in maintaining homeostasis. This course will introduce cytology, histology, and also focus on diseases related to the skeletal, nervous, sensory, muscular, endocrine, and reproductive systems. (4 units)

---

### BIOE 22 — Introduction to Cell and Molecular Bioengineering

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 or BIOL 1B. C orequisite : BIOE 22L

The aim of this course is to introduce students to fundamental concepts in cell and molecular biology. Topics covered in the course will include cellular structure and function, biological molecules, molecular mechanism of cellular function, cell proliferation and signaling. This course will also emphasize the importance of applications of genetic engineering in human health and diseases. Course will include lectures, peer reviewed papers, class discussion, short lab exercises, and team projects

### BIOE 23 — Introduction to Bio-Devices

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** MATH 14. Corequisite: BIOE 2 3L

**Corequisites:** BIOE 2 3L. (4 units)

This course covers the fundamentals of electronic circuits, with particular emphasis on connecting biosensors to analog-to-digital inputs of computers. This lab-based course introduces measuring, modeling, and designing electronic circuits.

### BIOE 100 — Bioengineering Research Seminar

Units: 1

A series of one-hour seminars will be presented by guest professors and researchers on their particular research topics in bioengineering or related fields. Students are required to attend four to five seminars and submit a one-page report summarizing the presentation for each seminar. May be repeated for credit up to two times. P/NP grading. Also listed as BIOE 200. (1 unit)

---

### BIOE 107 — Medical Device Product Development

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** Junior and Senior standing. (2 units)

The purpose of this course is to provide background information and knowledge to start or enhance a career in medical device product development. Discusses medical device examples, product development processes, regulation, industry information, and intellectual property. Also listed as BIOE 307 and EMGT 307.

---

### BIOE 108 — Biomedical Devices: Role of Polymers

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** Junior and Senior standing. (2 units)

This course is designed to highlight the role that polymers play in the design and fabrication of various medical devices ranging from simple intravenous drip systems to complex cardiac defibrillator implants and transcatheter heart valves. Topics include polymer basics, biocompatibility, biodegradation, and other tangentially related topics such as regulatory body approvals and intellectual property. Also listed as BIOE 208.

---

### BIOE 120 — Experimental Methods in Bioengineering

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** AMTH 106, BIOE 45 . (4 units)

This course will cover the principles of data representation, analysis, and experimental designs in bioreactors, biomaterials, and medical devices. Topics include error analyses, modeling, normality testing, hypothesis testing. Special emphasis will be placed on the interpretation of data from high-throughput assays used in “omics”/tissue engineering.

---

### BIOE 153 — Biomaterials Science

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** Prerequisites: BIOE 22 (or BIOL 1C) and BIOE 24. (4 units)

Basic principles of material properties, biomaterials categories, biomaterials engineering concepts and selected applications and practical aspects are taught in this class. This course is a foundation for an entry level medical device engineer or bioengineering advanced degree.

---

### BIOE 154 — Introduction to Biomechanics

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 24 . (4 units)

Engineering mechanics and applications in the analysis of human body movement, function, and injury. Review of issues related to designing devices for use in, or around, the human body including safety and biocompatibility .

---

### BIOE 155 — Biological Transport Phenomena

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** AMTH 106. (4 units)

The transport of mass, momentum, and energy are critical to the function of living systems and the design of medical devices. This course develops and applies scaling laws and the methods of continuum mechanics to biological transport phenomena over a range of length and time scales.

---

### BIOE 157 — Introduction to Biofuel Engineering

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 (or BIOL 1B), CHEM 12, PHYS 33. (2 units)

This course will cover the basic principles used to classify and evaluate biofuels in terms of thermodynamic and economic efficiencies as well as environmental impact for resource recovery. Special emphasis will be placed on emerging applications namely microbial fuel cell technology and photo-bioreactors. Also listed as BIOE 257/ENGR 257.

---

### BIOE 161 — Bioinstrumentation

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 (or BIOL 1B) and BIOE 23 . C orequisite : BIOE 161L

Transducers and biosensors from traditional to nanotechnology; bioelectronics and measurement system design; interface between biological system and instrumentation; data analysis; clinical safety. Laboratory components will include traditional clinical measurements and design and test of a measurement system with appropriate transducers.

### BIOE 162 — Signals and Systems for Bioengineers

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 45, BIOE 23, AMTH 106. C orequisite : BIOE 162L

Origin and characteristics of bioelectric, bio-optical, and bioacoustic signals generated from biological systems. Behavior and response of biological systems to stimulation. Acquisition and interpretation of signals. Signal processing methods include FFT spectral analysis and time-frequency analysis. Laboratory components will include modeling of signal generation and analysis of signals such as electrocardiogram (ECG), electromyogram (EMG), and vocal sound pressure waveforms.

### BIOE 168 — Biophotonics and Bioimaging

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 22 (or BIOL 1C) , CHEM 31, PHYS 33 and Junior and Senior standing . C orequisite : BIOE 168L

This course focuses on the interactions of light with biological matter and includes topics on the absorption of light by biomolecules, cells, and tissues, and the emission of light from these molecules via fluorescence and phosphorescence. The course will cover the application of biophotonics in cell biology, biotechnology, and biomedical imaging. Also listed as BIOE 268.

### BIOE 171 — Physiology and Anatomy for Engineers

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 (or BIOL 1B). C orequisite : BIOE 171L

Examines the structure and function of the human body and the mechanisms for maintaining homeostasis. The course will provide a molecular-level understanding of human anatomy and physiology in select organ systems. The course will include lectures, class discussions, case studies, computer simulations, field trips, lab exercises, and team projects.

### BIOE 172 — Introduction to Tissue Engineering

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 22 (or BIOL 1C). (4 units)

Introduces the basic principles underlying the design and engineering of functional biological substitutes to restore tissue function. Cell sourcing, manipulation of cell fate, biomaterial properties and cell-material interactions, and specific biochemical and biophysical cues presented by the extracellular matrix will be discussed, as well as the current status and future possibilities in the development of biological substitutes for various tissue types.

Microfluidics uses principles from a broad range of disciplines including fluid mechanics, material science, and optics, to achieve miniaturization and automation across a wide range of applications. This course introduces the fundamental physical and engineering concepts essential to microfluidics, with practical applications such as molecule and cell manipulation at the micro-scale. Through lectures and discussions of current literature, students will explore state-of-the-art microfluidic tech

### BIOE 175 — Biomolecular and Cellular Engineering I

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 22 (or BIOL 1C) and CHEM 31, or equivalent knowledge and by instructor’s permission. C orequisite : BIOE 175L

This course will focus on solving problems encountered in the design and manufacturing of biopharmaceutical products, including antibiotics, antibodies, protein drugs, and molecular biosensors, with particular emphasis on the principle and application of protein engineering and reprogramming cellular metabolic networks.

### BIOE 176 — Biomolecular and Cellular Engineering II

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 22 (or BIOL 1C) and CHEM 31, or equivalent knowledge and by instructor’s permission. BIOE 175 is recommended

This course will focus on the principle of designing, manufacturing synthetic materials and their biomedical and pharmaceutical applications. Emphasis of this class will be given to chemically synthetic materials such as polymers, and inorganic and organic compounds. Also listed as BIOE 226.

### BIOE 179 — Introduction to Neural Engineering

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 (or BIOL 1B). BIOE 171 recommended

This course provides a foundation in the neural principles underlying existing and upcoming neurotechnologies. The goal is to understand the design criteria necessary for engineering interventions in neural structure and function with application to neurological diseases, disorders, and injuries. Topics include brain imaging and stimulation, neural implants, nanotechnologies, stem cell and tissue engineering. This course includes lectures, literature critiques, and design projects. ### BIOE 180 — Clinical Trials: Design, Analysis and Ethical Issues

Units: 4

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 120 (or AMTH 108), or with consent of the instructor. (4 units)

This course will cover the principles behind the logistics of design and analysis of clinical trials from statistical and ethical perspectives. Topics include methods used for quantification of treatment effect(s) and associated bias interpretation, crossover designs used in randomized clinical trials, and clinical equipoise. Also listed as BIOE 2 80 .

### BIOE 185 — Physiology and Disease Biology

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** BIOE 21 (or BIOL 1B). BIOE 171 recommended

This course will provide a molecular-level understanding of physiology and disease biology, an overview of gastrointestinal diseases, and an introduction to medical devices used in diagnosis and treatment, as well as challenges in this field. This course will include lectures, class discussions, case studies, and team projects. Also listed as BIOE 285.

### BIOE 188 — Co-op Education

Units: 2

**Prerequisites:** Junior and Senior standing. and cumulative GPA ≥ 2

Integration of classroom study and practical experience in a planned program designed to give students practical work experience related to their academic field of study and career objectives. The course alternates (or parallels) periods of classroom study with periods of training in industry or government. Satisfactory completion of the work assignment includes preparation of a summary report on co-op activities. P/NP grading .

### BIOE 189 — Work Experience and Co-op Technical Report

Credit is given for a technical report on a specific activity, such as a design or research activity, after completing a co-op work assignment. Letter grades will be based on the content and quality of the report. May be taken more than once.
