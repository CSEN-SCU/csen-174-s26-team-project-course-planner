### CHEM 1 — Chemistry and the Environment

Units: 4

A survey of the role of chemistry in major environmental issues such as global warming, acid rain, ozone depletion, photochemical smog, persistent organic pollutants, fossil fuel, nuclear and renewable energy, recycling and environmental fate of pollutants. Laboratory 3 hours every other week. Students with prior credit for CHEM 11 can enroll only on a pass/no pass (P/NP) basis. (4 units)

---

### CHEM 5 — Chemistry: An Experimental Science

Units: 4

A survey of modern chemical applications, including applications to health, the environment, and consumer issues, and an introduction to the scientific method of inquiry. Laboratory 3 hours every other week. Cannot be taken by students with prior credit for CHEM 11 or 19 . (4 units)

### CHEM 101 — Bioinorganic Chemistry

Units: 5

**Corequisites:** CHEM 141 . (5 units)

Structure, properties, and reactivity of metal complexes and the function of metal ions in biological processes. Pre- or corequisite: CHEM 141 . (5 units)

### CHEM 102 — Inorganic Chemistry

Units: 5

**Corequisites:** CHEM 50, CHEM 33, and MATH 13 . (5 units)

Introduction to the bonding, structure, properties, and reactions of inorganic compounds, with examples drawn from main group and transition metal compounds. Laboratory 4 hours per week. Pre- or corequisites: CHEM 50, CHEM 33, and MATH 13 . (5 units)

---

### CHEM 103 — Environmental Chemistry

Units: 5

Sources, reactions, and transport of contaminants in soil, water, and air. Kinetic and thermodynamic models for smog formation, ozone layer depletion, acid rain, and the transport and degradation of contaminants in natural waters and soil, plus a brief look at global climate modeling. This course satisfies the Science, Technology and Society and Advanced Writing requirement. Pre requisite : CHEM 12, CTW 1 and 2 . (5 units)

---

### CHEM 111 — Instrumental Analysis

Units: 5

Principles and use of instrumentation. Focus on electronics, spectroscopic methods, mass spectrometry, and chemical separations. Laboratory 4 hours per week. Pre- or corequisite : CHEM 32 ; CHEM 50 recommended. (5 units)

### CHEM 115 — Chemistry and Biochemistry Seminar

### CHEM 130 — Organic Syntheses

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 33. (5 units)

Modern synthetic methods applied to the preparation of structurally complex target compounds, such as bioactive natural products and pharmaceuticals. Extensive discussion of synthetic planning, known as retrosynthetic analysis, emphasizing the standard bond - forming methods learned in CHEM 31–33.

---

### CHEM 131 — Bioorganic Chemistry

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 33. (5 units)

Chemical synthesis of carbohydrates, nucleic acids, peptides, proteins, and reaction mechanisms of biological cofactors.

---

### CHEM 132 — Polymer Chemistry

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** CTW 1 and 2, CHEM 33 ; or CHEM 32 with consent of instructor. (5 units)

Synthesis and characterization of polymers and complex macromolecules. Special emphasis on polymerization mechanisms, kinetic, and thermodynamic aspects of these reactions, and also applications of polymers in society. Much of the course content will come from current literature. This course satisfies the Advanced Writing requirement.

### CHEM 141 — Biochemistry I

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 12 and Pre- or corequisite: CHEM 33. (5 units)

**Corequisites:** CHEM 33. (5 units)

An introduction to structure/function relationships of biologically important molecules, enzymology, membrane biochemistry, and selected aspects of the intermediary metabolism of carbohydrates.

---

### CHEM 142 — Biochemistry II

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 141. (5 units)

Includes a study of various aspects of the intermediary metabolism of carbohydrates, lipids, and amino acids, as well as nucleic acid structure and function, protein synthesis, and subcellular sorting, and more advanced molecular physiology, including membrane biochemistry, signal transduction, and hormone action.

---

### CHEM 143 — Biochemical Techniques

Units: 3

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 141 and consent of instructor. (3 units)

A laboratory course emphasizing fundamental theory and practice in biochemical laboratory techniques, including preparation and handling of reagents; isolation, purification, and characterization of biomolecules; enzyme kinetics; spectrophotometric assays; and electrophoretic techniques. Laboratory 8 hours per week.

---

### CHEM 150 — Biophysical Chemistry

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** MATH 13 and CHEM 50 , or consent of instructor. (5 units)

Introduction to the physical behavior of biomolecules. Topics include transport properties, reaction kinetics, sedimentation, electrophoresis, binding dynamics, and molecular motion.

---

### CHEM 152 — Chemical Thermodynamics

Units: 5

**Prerequisites:** MATH 13 and CHEM 50 . (5 units)

Fundamental laws of thermodynamics, and applications to ideal and real gas equations of state, ideal and real solutions, phase equilibria, and electrochemistry.

---

### CHEM 154 — Physical Chemistry Laboratory

Units: 3

**Prerequisites:** MATH 13 and CHEM 50 . It is recommended that students are enrolled in or have completed CHEM 151 or 152

Experimental applications of thermodynamics, kinetics, spectroscopy, and other aspects of physical chemistry. Laboratory 8 hours per week.

---

### CHEM 182 — Undergraduate Research

**Prerequisites:** Consent of instructor. (1–3 units)

Experimental research project supervised by chemistry and biochemistry faculty members. Each unit requires a minimum of 30 hours of laboratory work. May be repeated for credit.

---

### CHEM 183 — Senior Research Experience

Units: 3

**Prerequisites:** Senior standing in chemistry and consent of instructor. (3 units)

Individual research under the supervision of chemistry and biochemistry faculty members, culminating in a comprehensive progress report. Laboratory at least 9 hours per week.

---

### CHEM 184 — Capstone Research Experience

Units: 3

**Prerequisites:** CHEM 182 or 183, and consent of instructor. (3 units)

Continuation of individual research under the supervision of a chemistry and biochemistry faculty member, culminating in a thesis and oral presentation. Laboratory at least 9 hours per week.
